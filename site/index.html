Efficiently Learning Robust Torque-based Locomotion Through Reinforcement with Model-Based Supervision

Yashuai Yan, Tobias Egle, Christian Ott, Dongheui Lee

A residual reinforcement learning approach for robust bipedal locomotion, combining a model-based planner and whole-body controller with oracle-supervised corrective actions.

arXiv IEEE PDF

Abstract

We propose a control framework that integrates model-based bipedal locomotion with residual reinforcement learning to achieve robust and adaptive walking in the presence of real-world uncertainties. Our approach leverages a model-based controller, comprising a Divergent Component of Motion trajectory planner and a whole-body controller, as a reliable base policy.

To address inaccurate dynamics modeling and sensor noise, we introduce a residual policy trained through reinforcement learning with domain randomization. Crucially, we employ a model-based oracle policy with privileged access to ground-truth dynamics during training to supervise the residual policy via a supervised loss.

How it works

Model-based base policy, residual corrections, oracle supervision.

The base controller handles nominal locomotion while the residual policy focuses on the deviations introduced by imperfect dynamics and noisy sensing. During training, a privileged oracle provides a supervisory signal so the residual policy learns corrective behaviors efficiently.

Why it matters

More robustness with less reward shaping.

The method is positioned as a practical route to sim-to-real transfer for torque-based bipedal locomotion, where domain randomization and supervisory guidance reduce the need for extensive manual reward engineering.
